UCLM Microcredential on AI and Geospatial Technologies for Forest Ecosystem Restoration: María Burguet, Héctor Nieto and Benjamin Mary

by Benjamin Mary | 2026/09/08

The Microcredencial en IA y Tecnologías Geoespaciales para Restauración de Ecosistemas Forestales, run online by UCLM (E.T.S. Ingeniería Agronómica y de Montes y Biotecnología, Albacete) from 14 April to 14 May 2026, brought together researchers from Tech4Agro (ICA-CSIC) to teach a hands-on program on GIS, geophysics, remote sensing and AI applied to forest ecosystems affected by fires and drought. The course combined lectures, Jupyter Notebook exercises, and practical field/data sessions, and closed with participant surveys (encuestas) gathering feedback on the sessions. The course was a success, with ~15 participants completing the program.

María Burguet — Linking Terrain Geometry with the Ecological Function

María Burguet (Tech4Agro, ICA-CSIC) led sessions focused on the geomorphological and hydrological characterization of the landscape with practical and interdisciplinary “learning-by-doing” approaches. The activities included the use of 5-meter digital terrain models (DTMs) derived from PNOA LiDAR data through GIS and programming tools (QGIS, ArcGIS Pro, and R) to provide quantitative criteria to prioritize conservation and restoration actions in ecosystems.

Héctor Nieto — Thermal Infrared Remote Sensing and Evapotranspiration

Héctor Nieto (Tech4Agro, ICA-CSIC), coordinator of the microcredential, guided participants through the theory and practice of surface temperature retrieval and land energy flux modeling using thermal infrared satellite imagery. Sessions included hands-on notebooks and datasets for testing evapotranspiration algorithms in real conditions.

Benjamin Mary — Linking Geophysics and Hydrological Modeling

Benjamin Mary (ICA-CSIC) presented on integrating geophysical observations with hydrological modeling workflows, connecting the post-fire geophysics content to broader catchment-scale water accounting approaches developed in projects such as GRWATER.

Participant Feedback

At the end of the course, participants completed a set of surveys (encuestas) reflecting on their prior experience with post-fire environments and geophysics, their understanding of the methods presented, and their interest in applying geophysics in practice going forward. Feedback was positive overall, confirming the course met its goal of introducing participants to thermal remote sensing and post-fire geophysics techniques.
